02 / HISTORY

A short history of flight

Follow the moments that moved aircraft from experiment to everyday technology.

EPQ ARTEFACTHomeHistoryPhysicsFuture
1000 BCE1000 BCEKitesKites were invented in China, the first man-made objects to fly.
852 BCE852 BCEDeath Due To CuriosityThe English King Blaudud is reportedly killed attempting to fly.
9th Century CE9th Century CEHeavier Than AirAbbas Ibn Firnas makes what some believe to be the first attempt at heavier than air flight in aviation history.
1010 CE1010 CEGlidersEilmer of Malmesbury, an English monk, pilots a primitive gliding craft from the tower of Malmesbury Abbey.
1489-15001489-1500Drawings Of The FutureLeonardo da Vinci designs flying machines; his drawings stay hidden until 400 years after his death.
17091709ModelsBartolomeu Laurenço de Gusmão designs a model glider.
17821782ExperimentsJoseph Michel and Jacques Étienne Montgolfier begin the first aeronautical experiments leading to the hot air balloon.
17831783The Paris BaloonThe first untethered manned hot air balloon flight takes place in Paris in a Montgolfier balloon.
17991799WingsGeorge Cayley establishes the modern notion of the fixed-wing aircraft.
18431843DesignsCayley's biplane design is published.
18531853Manned GliderCayley's glider is safely flown by his servant, the first recorded successful manned glider flight.
1870s1870sWing EngineeringOtto Lilienthal undertakes the most important wing-design studies since Cayley.
1890-18961890-1896Monoplane And Biplanes GlidersLilienthal flies monoplane and biplane gliders, completing as many as 2,000 flights before his fatal crash in August 1896.
19021902Controllable Glider- The Wright brothers complete their first fully controllable glider, the breakthrough toward powered flight; Wilbur banks at Kill Devil Hills on 24 October.
19031903First Powered FlightThe Wright brothers make the first recorded powered, sustained, controlled flight in a heavier-than-air machine, Orville piloting; fewer than 10 people witness it, and the roughly 120 ft flown is less than a Boeing 747's wingspan.
19041904Record BreakingNew Zealander Richard Pearse makes his first recorded powered flight; evidence indicates he did not achieve controlled flight before the Wrights.
19061906Powered Flight In EuropeAlberto Santos-Dumont conducts the first widely recognized public powered flights in Paris, the first powered flight in Europe.
19081908Time And Distance- The Wrights' aircraft can fly over two hours and cover more than 75 miles.
19101910Women In FlightRaymonde de Laroche becomes the first woman in the world to hold a pilot's license.
19111911American History And CompetitionHarriet Quimby becomes the first American woman licensed pilot; the Daily Mail offers £10,000 for the first transatlantic flight; George Bolt launches his own glider above Christchurch and takes New Zealand's first aerial photographs.
19191919TransatlanticJohn Alcock and Arthur Brown make the first non-stop transatlantic flight (Newfoundland to Ireland) in a Vickers Vimy, 15 hours 57 minutes, also the first transatlantic airmail flight; they win £10,000 and are knighted.
19251925EnginesThe Wright J-5 (R790) Whirlwind radial engine debuts, one of the first reliable aircraft engines.
19271927Solo And Non-StopCharles Lindbergh makes the first solo non-stop transatlantic flight, New York to Paris, about 3,600 miles in 33.5 hours.
19291929Gas TurbineFrank Whittle begins work on his centrifugal gas turbine designs.
19301930Jet EngineFrank Whittle is credited with inventing the jet engine.
19321932Woman Made HistoryAmelia Earhart becomes the first woman to fly the Atlantic solo and non-stop, repeating Lindbergh's journey on its fifth anniversary.
1932-19371932-1937Around the WorldJean Batten makes record-breaking flights around the world.
19351935Turbojet Patent FiledHans von Ohain files a patent for his turbojet concept.
19371937First Practical Jet EngineWhittle produces his first practical jet engine.
19391939License and Jet FirstsWilla Brown becomes the first African American woman to earn a pilot's license; later that year Germany's Heinkel He 178 makes the first jet-powered aircraft flight.
19421942America's First TurbojetThe Bell P-59 Airacomet flies but never sees combat.
19441944Operational Jet FightersThe first operational turbojet fighters enter service: the Gloster Meteor and the Messerschmitt Me 262.
19471947The Sound Barrier FallsCharles Yeager pilots the Bell X-1, the first aircraft to exceed the speed of sound in level flight.
19491949First Commercial Jet BuiltThe de Havilland Comet, the world's first commercial jet aircraft, is built.
19521952Jet Travel BeginsThe Comet launches commercial jet air travel; its structural failures later advance fatigue and certification methods.
19531953First Turboprop ServiceThe Vickers Viscount inaugurates the world's first turboprop airline service.
19581958The Jet Age Goes GlobalThe Boeing 707 enters service, establishing global commercial jet travel at roughly Mach 0.8.
Early 1960sEarly 1960sThe Trijet ArrivesThe Boeing 727, a rear-mounted trijet with a T-tail, is introduced.
Late 1960sLate 1960sThe Workhorse JetThe Boeing 737 is introduced and becomes one of the most successful commercial jetliners ever built.
19691969Supersonic and Jumbo FirstsFirst flights of the supersonic transports (TU-144 and Concorde); the Boeing 747 is introduced, revolutionizing long-haul travel.
19701970747 Commercial ServiceThe Boeing 747 makes its first commercial flight.
19761976Concorde Carries PassengersConcorde begins its first passenger-carrying service.
19791979Pedal Power Across the ChannelBryan Allen pedals the Gossamer Albatross across the English Channel, breaking the human-powered flight distance record.
19861986Round the World Non-StopDick Rutan and Jeana Yeager fly the Voyager around the world non-stop in 9 days.
19881988Fly-by-Wire EraThe Airbus A320 debuts with digital fly-by-wire controls and advanced composite materials.
19961996Concorde's Record RunConcorde sets the New York to London record: 2 hours 52 minutes 59 seconds at up to 1,354 mph.
20032003Concorde RetiredConcorde is retired after decades of supersonic passenger service.
2005-20062005-2006Solo Around the WorldSteve Fossett makes the first non-stop solo round-the-world flight (2005), then a 26,389.3-mile flight landing in England (2006).
20092009Glider Record BrokenTerry Delore breaks the world gliding record by 100 km, travelling 2,400 km within New Zealand.
20112011Jetpacks and Flying CarsThe Martin jetpack reaches 5,000 ft in an unmanned test; the Terrafugia Transition flying car debuts.
20162016Solar-Powered Globe TrotSolar Impulse 2 becomes the first renewable-energy-powered plane to tour the globe.
20192019Electric Aviation ArrivesThe world's first fully-electric commercial aircraft completes a test in Canada in December.
20212021End of an Icon, Electric MilestoneAirbus A380 production ends after 251 aircraft; Gary Freedman flies New Zealand's electric plane from Blenheim to Wellington on 1 November.
20232023Electric Cargo OrderAir New Zealand orders one Alia electric plane, to fly cargo between Wellington and Marlborough from 2026.
28 Oct 202528 Oct 2025Quiet Supersonic First FlightThe Lockheed Martin X-59 QueSST makes its first flight.
5 Jun 20265 Jun 2026Quieter Than a Sonic BoomThe X-59 exceeds Mach 1 for the first time.
